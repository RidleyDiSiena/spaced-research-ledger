Where the Cryptography Comes From

This component does not implement everything it is asked about. What it inherits, from what, and at which version:

  • Apple CryptoKit and corecrypto – platform. Inherits: FIPS 140-3. The networking frameworks do no cryptography of their own; corecrypto does, and corecrypto is what Apple submits for validation. The FIPS answer here is therefore CryptoKit and corecrypto’s. [2]

FIPS 140-3

Apple Network framework and URLSession holds no FIPS 140-3 certificate of its own. It takes its cryptography from Apple CryptoKit and corecrypto (platform), and Apple CryptoKit and corecrypto holds no certificate of its own. Whether a given algorithm is inside that validated boundary is Apple CryptoKit and corecrypto’s answer, not this component’s: see the Apple CryptoKit and corecrypto page. Running in an approved mode also needs the validated version on a tested operating environment, which no register can tell you.

Notes

  • ML-KEM, TLS 1.3: default is the whole point of this cell and is unusual in this register: an application that does nothing at all gets post-quantum key exchange, because the frameworks turn it on for everything built against them. That is a stronger claim than any library’s default-group list, since it reaches software whose authors never considered the question.

Known Unknowns

Cells an engineer would ask about that no cited source has settled yet. not checked means nobody has searched. searching means fewer than 2 passes are done. none found means the search was completed and came back empty, and the artifacts examined are named; a result older than 90 days is marked for recheck.

  • x25519mlkem768 / tls – searching, 1 of 2 passes: Apple states that quantum-secure TLS is on by default but does not name the negotiated group, and this register records hybrid groups one row per NAMED group. Recording X25519MLKEM768 on the strength of it being the group everyone else deploys would be exactly the plausible-looking guess the register refuses. Second pass: Apple developer documentation or a WWDC session naming the group, or a packet capture, which is evidence of a different kind. Examined: Apple Platform Security, Quantum-secure cryptography in Apple operating systems.
