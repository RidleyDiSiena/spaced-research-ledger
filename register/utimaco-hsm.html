Notes

  • ML-KEM, Primitive: An application package, not base firmware – but an in-field upgrade rather than a hardware exchange, which is the distinction that matters to somebody with devices already deployed. Still available rather than experimental: it is a shipped product with a free evaluation simulator, not a preview channel.
  • ML-KEM, PKCS#11: AVAILABLE, WITH A CAVEAT THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT OF THIS COLUMN: the mechanisms are vendor-defined, not the standard PKCS#11 ones. Software written against Utimaco’s mechanism numbers does not move to another vendor’s device unchanged, which is exactly the portability question a reader asks of this column and cannot ask of primitive. Compare Thales (standard Cryptoki mechanisms) and Entrust (ML-KEM not in PKCS#11 at all).
  • ML-DSA, PKCS#11: Vendor-defined mechanisms, as the ML-KEM cell.
  • SLH-DSA, Primitive: TWO ENUMERATIONS OF THE SAME PACKAGE, neither containing it. The Quantum Protect product page says it “offers the lattice-based algorithms ML-KEM and ML-DSA and the stateful hash-based signature schemes LMS and XMSS (including their multitree variants HSS and XMSS-MT)”, and the simulator page lists exactly the same six for evaluation: “ML-KEM, ML-DSA, LMS, HSS, XMSS, and XMSS-MT”. Both are lists of what the package contains, written to be complete, and SLH-DSA is in neither. The validated module’s security policy is silent on every post-quantum algorithm, which is a different fact – that module predates the Quantum Protect package rather than excluding SLH-DSA specifically. Note the shape of Utimaco’s choice: it is the STATEFUL hash-based schemes it covers most thoroughly, LMS, HSS, XMSS and XMSS-MT, while omitting the stateless one.
  • LMS / HSS, Firmware signing: The security policy for certificate 5223 describes the only route by which firmware can be loaded: the image “is accompanied by a valid ECDSA signature (i.e., passes ECDSA signature verification algorithm Cert. #A6714) corresponding to the Image Signing Key ISK+”. ECDSA rather than RSA, and classical either way – notable because Utimaco’s Quantum Protect package offers customers LMS, HSS, XMSS and XMSS-MT, none of which signs the package itself. THE PATTERN ACROSS THIS CATEGORY, now evidenced rather than suspected: the hardware security modules that hold FIPS-validated post-quantum algorithms FOR CUSTOMERS still sign their OWN firmware classically. Thales Luna T7 uses RSA-4096, Entrust nShield 5s uses RSA-4096, Utimaco uses ECDSA. Crypto4A is the exception and dual-signs with HSS. This is exactly what the firmware column was added to expose: a module whose customer keys are post-quantum and whose root of trust is RSA has moved the problem rather than solved it, and the firmware signing key is the longest-lived key in the device.
  • XMSS / XMSS^MT, Primitive: One of only two components in the register with XMSS at all – the other is Crypto4A. Thales’s competing package names LMS-HSS only, so this looks like a real product difference rather than a documentation gap.
