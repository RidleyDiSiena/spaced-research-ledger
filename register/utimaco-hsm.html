Notes

  • ML-KEM, Primitive: An application package, not base firmware – but an in-field upgrade rather than a hardware exchange, which is the distinction that matters to somebody with devices already deployed. Still available rather than experimental: it is a shipped product with a free evaluation simulator, not a preview channel.
  • ML-KEM, PKCS#11: AVAILABLE, WITH A CAVEAT THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT OF THIS COLUMN: the mechanisms are vendor-defined, not the standard PKCS#11 ones. Software written against Utimaco’s mechanism numbers does not move to another vendor’s device unchanged, which is exactly the portability question a reader asks of this column and cannot ask of primitive. Compare Thales (standard Cryptoki mechanisms) and Entrust (ML-KEM not in PKCS#11 at all).
  • ML-DSA, PKCS#11: Vendor-defined mechanisms, as the ML-KEM cell.
  • XMSS / XMSS^MT, Primitive: One of only two components in the register with XMSS at all – the other is Crypto4A. Thales’s competing package names LMS-HSS only, so this looks like a real product difference rather than a documentation gap.

Known Unknowns

Cells an engineer would ask about that no cited source has settled yet. not checked means nobody has searched. searching means fewer than 2 passes are done. none found means the search was completed and came back empty, and the artifacts examined are named; a result older than 90 days is marked for recheck.

  • slh-dsa / primitive – searching, 1 of 2 passes: Quantum Protect’s description enumerates what it offers – ML-KEM, ML-DSA, LMS, HSS, XMSS, XMSS-MT – and SLH-DSA is not among them. That reads like an enumeration, which would make this a cited none, but it is marketing copy for a package rather than a mechanism list, and the register’s rule is that a none needs a source that means to be exhaustive. Second pass: the Quantum Protect data sheet or the simulator’s mechanism list. Examined: Utimaco Quantum Protect product page, Utimaco Quantum Protect simulator page.
  • lms-hss / firmware – searching, 1 of 2 passes: Whether Utimaco’s own firmware images are signed with a post-quantum algorithm is unstated in the pages read. The Quantum Protect package is itself described as a loadable firmware module, which makes the question sharper rather than softer: what signs the loadable module? Second pass: the u.trust Anchor security policy for certificate 5223. Examined: Utimaco Quantum Protect product page.
