Notes

  • X25519MLKEM768, TLS 1.3: TWO SEPARATE LEGS, and this cell is the origin-facing one – Cloudflare to the customer’s own server, which is the leg an operator controls least and notices last. The browser-facing leg is also post-quantum and is measured rather than asserted: Cloudflare reports over a third of human-generated traffic to its network using TLS 1.3 with X25519MLKEM768. default is exact here: nobody configured it, and the same rollout cut HelloRetryRequests on origin connections from roughly 52% to 3.7%, which is the sort of operational consequence a register of capabilities usually cannot show.
  • ML-DSA, TLS 1.3: THE SIGNATURE HALF, ON THE ONE LEG WHERE IT IS POSSIBLE TODAY. “Authenticated Origin Pulls and Custom Origin Trust Store products now support post-quantum (PQ) authentication via Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm (ML-DSA) signatures to protect connections between Cloudflare and customer origin servers.” And Cloudflare says plainly why this leg and not the other: “the unique requirements of the Cloudflare-to-origin connection have allowed us to deploy post-quantum authentication via ML-DSA ahead of support landing in the WebPKI for the public Internet” – the origin leg can use a custom PKI, so it escapes the certificate ecosystem that is holding everyone else up. The browser-facing leg is waiting on the WebPKI, where Cloudflare is working with others at the IETF on Merkle Tree Certificates, targeting initial deployments in 2027. available rather than default: it is a product feature a customer turns on, free on all plans, not something every zone gets.
