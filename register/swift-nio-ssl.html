Where the Cryptography Comes From

This component does not implement everything it is asked about. What it inherits, from what, and at which version:

  • BoringSSL – vendored, pinned at BoringSSL commit 817ab07ebb53da35afea409ab9328f578492832d. Inherits: FIPS 140-3. The package vendors BoringSSL as CNIOBoringSSL rather than linking it, and Package.swift records the exact commit. A BoringSSL release therefore does NOT reach swift-nio-ssl until the project re-vendors, which is why the pin is the load-bearing part of this record. The TLS surface is swift-nio-ssl’s own and is not inherited. [2] [3]

FIPS 140-3

swift-nio-ssl holds no FIPS 140-3 certificate of its own. It takes its cryptography from BoringSSL (vendored, at BoringSSL commit 817ab07ebb53da35afea409ab9328f578492832d), and BoringSSL holds FIPS 140-3 certificates 5296, 5244, 5104, 4735, 4444, 4407, 4156, and has 3 modules in the CMVP queue. Whether a given algorithm is inside that validated boundary is BoringSSL’s answer, not this component’s: see the BoringSSL page. Running in an approved mode also needs the validated version on a tested operating environment, which no register can tell you.
